Nine Tides over Vellamar

Long before the maps, this coast was a country. It sank in one night — not from war, but because it forgot its own name. The sea keeps what it takes, yet keeps it badly: every tide it tries to hand the kingdom back, grain by grain, and every tide it thinks better of it and pulls the whole thing under. A tidewright is someone who catches the grains before the water changes its mind.

        How to Build

        The one law

        Dry sand cannot stand. Every grain has an angle it refuses to exceed. Dry, that angle is about 33° — a slope, never a wall. Wet it and the water bridges the grains: you can go past vertical. Wet it too much and it liquefies and runs. The sweet spot is damp, not soaked.

        Then pat it. Compaction is the second half of strength — packed sand resists both collapse and the sea. A patted, damp wall will outlive an unpacked one by whole tides.

        The pail

        Sand is conserved. Digging fills your pail; pouring empties it. You are not making sand — you are moving the coast around. Dig your moat where you want a moat and the spoil becomes your keep.

        The sea

        Waves break where the water goes thin, and they eat the base first. Undermine a wall and the wall falls on its own — that is the simulation, not a script. A moat spends a wave's energy; a seawall (a long, low, packed berm on the seaward side) turns it. Build both.

        Water flows downhill and carries sand with it. A moat that drains landward will carve your own castle out from under you.

        How it plays
        • The tide is the drawdown. The water sits at the price’s recent peak. Fall 30% from it and the sea reaches the high-water line and takes the loose sand first.
        • Your bag is your pail. Connect a wallet and every 0.1% of supply you hold is another 4 m³ of sand to build with.
        • Buys draw the water back. Sells send a wave in. Graduation locks the pool — and the sea goes slack for good.
        • Remembrance is what you had standing above the high-water line, weighted by how well you packed it.
